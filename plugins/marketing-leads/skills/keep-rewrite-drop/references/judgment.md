# Judgment

Rules from my own content clean-ups. Apply a rule only when its trigger is in the data, and when you use one, say so on the sheet in plain words, so the reader can follow the reasoning and disagree with it.

## 1. One lead is a signal, not proof

**Trigger:** a page has leads in only one or two months.
**Decision:** don't protect it like a steady page. Ask where the lead came from first.
**Why:** a single lead often comes from a campaign link, a newsletter or sales sending the page, not from search. Protecting it on that basis keeps pages that don't earn their place.
**Doesn't apply:** when the site gets very few leads overall (under about 20 a year). Then every lead page is worth a closer look; say the numbers are small.

## 2. The steady pages are the template

**Trigger:** group 1 has at least two pages.
**Decision:** before any rewrite, name what the steady pages share, and make every rewrite copy it.
**Why:** the pages that bring leads month after month already show what the buyers respond to. A rewrite that ignores them starts from a guess.
**Doesn't apply:** when the steady pages are all brand or homepage traffic. Then there is no format to copy yet; say so.

## 3. Page one with almost no clicks is an AI Overview problem, not a writing problem

**Trigger:** a page ranks in the top 10 for its searches and gets very few clicks, and those searches show an AI Overview.
**Decision:** don't promise the rewrite brings clicks back. Decide on the page's value to buyers, not on its lost clicks.
**Why:** on one software company's blog I worked on, half the impressions came from page-one searches with an AI Overview on top, and those searches brought almost no clicks. The answer is given before anyone reaches the page, wherever the page lives.
**Doesn't apply:** when there is no AI Overview on those searches. Then low clicks on page one is a title, snippet or intent problem.

## 4. Cutting lowers traffic and is still right

**Trigger:** a large share of clicks goes to pages in group 3, or to group 2 pages that will be dropped, about topics the buyers don't search.
**Decision:** drop or merge them anyway, and say upfront that total traffic will fall.
**Why:** at a workplace hardware company, removing consumer articles that drew most of the clicks and none of the buyers halved total clicks, and clicks on every other page grew. At a software company's relaunch, cutting indexed pages by about three-quarters came before weekly organic trials grew fivefold.
**Doesn't apply:** when those pages bring leads. Then they are group 1, whatever their topic.

## 5. A rewrite for AI is a rewrite for the buyer

**Trigger:** the person, the client or the CEO asks to rewrite content "for AI", "for ChatGPT" or "for GEO".
**Decision:** ask which pages answered a real buyer question. Those get the test. The rest go to merge or drop.
**Why:** AI assistants search before they answer and pull from the pages that rank for the buyer's question. Pages written for those questions get retrieved without separate AI tactics.
**Doesn't apply:** never. There is no separate AI rewrite.
