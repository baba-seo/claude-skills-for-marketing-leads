# The six causes

When a B2B website gets organic visits and no pipeline, the cause is almost always one of these six. Each finding from the checks points to one of them.

| # | Cause | Checks that confirm it | Signs | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Visitors who will never buy | 1, 3 | Most top queries are definitions, jobs, logins or how-tos for people outside the market; a large share of clicks from countries they don't sell in | Stop judging the site on total visits. Write for the questions buyers ask, even when few people search them |
| 2 | Clicks mostly from people who already know them | 2 | Brand clicks are most of the total; non-brand clicks are small or flat | The non-brand group is what organic brings on its own. If it's small, the site lacks pages for the problems and categories buyers search before they know the company |
| 3 | No page for a buyer close to a decision | 4 | Pricing, comparison, industry or customer-story pages are missing | Build the missing pages first, starting with the comparison buyers make most often |
| 4 | A hidden path to sales | 5, 6 | More than one or two steps to contact sales on a phone; several competing calls to action; a form that doesn't reach the CRM or arrives with the wrong source | One clear next step per page, and a form that lands in the CRM with the right source |
| 5 | Slow replies to leads | 7 | First reply takes hours or days, or nobody owns website leads | Route website leads to one named person, with an alert, and a calendar link in the confirmation so a buyer can book without waiting |
| 6 | Leads the report can't see | 8, 9 | Leads say they found the company through search or an AI assistant, and the CRM says "direct" or "unknown" | Report what is known with a label on it (fact, estimate, assumption), and ask every new lead how they first heard of the company |

## Order of fixes

Default order, cheapest first, because the cheap fixes work on leads they already get:

1. Slow replies (5): a routing rule and an alert. This week.
2. Hidden path to sales (4): a few days of page and form fixes.
3. Leads the report can't see (6): ten questions to new leads, then a monthly habit.
4. No page for buyers close to a decision (3): a few weeks per page.
5. Wrong visitors, or only people who know them (1 and 2): months of new pages, so it comes last.

Change the order only when their data gives a reason, and say what the reason is. Example: if replies are already under 5 minutes, cause 5 is ruled out and drops off the list.

## When nothing is confirmed

If every check comes back clean, say so plainly. Then look at the numbers again: are there simply too few non-brand buyer searches for the leads they expect? That is a demand question, not a website problem, and the honest answer is that more pages for buyer questions will take months to change it.
